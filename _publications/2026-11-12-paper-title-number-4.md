Spatial omics enables the integration of high-dimensional molecular organization with clinical outcomes, yet incorporating spatial single-cell information into predictive models at the population scale remains challenging. SPIN (spatial predictive integration network) integrates subject-specific, cell-type-level, spatially informed co-expression or co-abundance networks for population-scale clinical prediction. Its workflow combines network construction, molecular-to-clinical prediction by Bayesian scalar-on-network regression with manifold learning, and visualization and interpretation. Applications to spatial transcriptomics and spatial proteomics data demonstrate clinical prediction while identifying shared subnetworks, hub genes, and biological pathways associated with disease outcomes.
